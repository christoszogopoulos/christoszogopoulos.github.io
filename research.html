Working Paper

Mobilisation Episodes and Fiscal Capacity in the Long 19th Century

with Pantelis Kammas & Andreas Kakridis

Abstract: “War made the state, and the state made war.” (Tilly, 1975). This famous dictum captures the essence of the bellicist thesis, which argues that military conflicts have been the primary catalyst for the development of fiscal state capacity. This paper examines the relationship between public revenue and episodes of military mobilization in a panel of 25 European and American countries from 1816 to 1913. The key methodological innovation of our analysis lies in identifying episodes of military mobilization by detecting sharp increases in military spending, rather than by relying on the occurrence of actual war events. This approach enables one to identify periods of military buildup that can drive increased fiscal effort, even in the absence of actual military conflict. In contrast to previous empirical studies that focus on the fiscal impact of war events and yield largely inconclusive results, our analysis offers clear and definitive empirical evidence. More specifically, the empirical findings indicate that episodes of mobilization result in a substantial rise in public revenue, with evidence suggesting a lasting long-term effect. These findings imply that it is the preparation for conflict — not just active warfare — that can drive states to enhance their fiscal capacity.

Courses

Microeconomics II — Teaching Assistant, 2024–present

Main professor: Vasia Panousi, Associate Professor of Economics

Course description: This is an intermediate microeconomics course, building directly on Microeconomics I, with emphasis on the mathematical presentation of its concepts and tools. It covers consumer theory under certainty — preferences, utility maximization, expenditure minimization, Marshallian and Hicksian demand, duality, and the Slutsky equation — the theory of the firm and production, including cost and profit maximization and returns to scale, and market structures ranging from perfect competition to monopoly, covering consumer and producer surplus, deadweight loss, and policy interventions such as taxes, subsidies, and price controls. The course also introduces consumer choice under uncertainty (expected utility, risk aversion, insurance, and intertemporal choice) and closes with an introduction to behavioral economics, including loss aversion, overconfidence, and hyperbolic preferences.

Microeconomics I — Teaching Assistant, 2023–2024

Main professor: Chrysovalantou (Valanta) Milliou, Professor of Economics

Course description: This is an introductory course in microeconomic theory, presenting its fundamental concepts, tools, and conclusions. It aims to help students understand these concepts, become familiar with the relevant tools, and apply them to analyze a range of issues related to the functioning of the real economy. Topics covered include supply and demand, consumer choice, production costs and profit, perfectly competitive markets and their efficiency, market interventions, imperfectly competitive markets — monopoly, monopolistic competition, and oligopoly — market failures, and factor markets.

Macroeconomics I — Teaching Assistant, 2023–2024

Main professors: Panagiotis Konstantinou, Associate Professor of International Macroeconomics, and George Economides, Professor of Economics

Course description: This course introduces the fundamental concepts of macroeconomic theory. It covers the measurement of key macroeconomic aggregates — GDP, consumption, investment, inflation, and unemployment — and the various approaches used to identify their main determinants, explaining why they change and how fiscal or monetary policy can affect their evolution. The course equips students with the core methodological tools of modern macroeconomic analysis, enabling them to interpret macroeconomic developments in both Greece and the global economy, and to anticipate the effects of specific economic policy measures.

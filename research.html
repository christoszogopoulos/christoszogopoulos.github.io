Mobilisation Episodes and Fiscal Capacity in the Long 19th Century

with Pantelis Kammas & Andreas Kakridis

Abstract: This paper examines how large military buildups shape state fiscal capacity. Using Local Projections Difference-in-Differences (LP-DiD) on an unbalanced panel of 25 European and Latin American countries over 1816–1913, we show that large-scale mobilisation episodes do not raise fiscal capacity on impact, but generate positive and persistent gains in the medium and long run — consistent with a "ratchet effect" in which wartime tax and administrative institutions outlast the conflicts that created them.

Question: How do large military buildups influence state fiscal capacity?

Finding: Using Local Projections Difference-in-Differences (LP-DiD) on an unbalanced panel of 25 European and Latin American countries, 1816–1913, we find that large military buildups do not raise fiscal capacity immediately, but generate positive and persistent gains in the medium and long run.
